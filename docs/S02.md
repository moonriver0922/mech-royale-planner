## Required behavior

1. Weapon decisions use current observation values and `stats` with official configuration fallbacks. Laser hold/charge/release, minimum charge, cooldown, hold timeout, short effect, slot changes and dropped observations have explicit state. Releasing before minimum charge queues a later shot: it is not a cancellation. After an intentional release keep that slot false until observed reset/cooldown; no rapid re-press during queued release.
2. Pick and approach legal, visible ground laser loot with an empty secondary slot using the existing resource planner. Retain lightning availability. Rank candidates by existing travel/equip opportunity cost, use `max(equip_s, weapon cooldown)` for newly supported equipment, and send `take` only from the current reported horizontal radius and vertical limit. No predictive early pickup, unsupported roof route, equipment replacement, or forced detour during urgent zone/recovery.
3. Use one combat target. Distinguish gun projectile intercept aim from laser hitscan aim. Decide the final shared aim before either trigger; actual torso alignment and a bounded next-physics-tick aim prediction must justify a discharge. Laser release cannot silently use the gun's lead point. Gun fire remains possible when its own geometry is legal during charging; a necessary short laser-alignment handoff may suppress incompatible gun shots. Do not wait arbitrarily long at full charge.
4. Check finite shot segment, current range, target dimensions, vertical limits, muzzle clearance and static obstruction. Recompute at actual release, and never assume a commanded aim is already achieved. Use observed target positions, not invented hidden positions. Official config supplies rates and timings.
5. Charging reduces movement speed. Avoid starting a charge when it threatens an urgent zone exit or recovery; emergency movement can release the charge. Zone planning must account for the current charging speed until release/queued release is actually resolved. Bound and explain any approximate movement prediction.
6. Keep recovery and environmental priorities intact. Do not fight solely because carrying a laser; no forced damage that prevents a valid safe recovery. Any queueable tail shot is acknowledged by state and subsequent event evidence.
7. Keep runtime well below the 50 ms decision budget. No dependencies, no online learning, no full game tree search. Deterministic tie-breaking and bounded geometry work.

## Implemented decisions

The ground resource planner considers a visible laser alongside cubes and lightning. The first ready weapon that can attack a visible actor participates in the existing engagement priority. A laser can begin its charge during a gun reload. Target ranking uses time to minimum useful charge. This keeps a valid charge opportunity ahead of an unrelated nearby resource when the torso is already close to the target.

The laser aims at the observed actor's vertical midpoint. A finite muzzle ray reaches the first point of contact with its cylinder, with map clearance tested up to that contact. Ordinary release requires both the observed ray and a one-physics-tick estimate to intersect. That estimate slews toward the final shared command and translates both actors with their reported velocity. One decision interval of linear motion can flag a closing shot window for an early release. Acceleration, collisions after the observation and intervening dynamic actors remain sources of error.

The gun retains its projectile lead while its own ray is usable and the charge has enough time for one gun decision plus the larger slew from the actual and gun-commanded bearings to laser aim. Both weapons share one actor and the final torso command. A full charge is released on the first aligned frame. If alignment or temporary cover prevents that, the controller allows up to two further decision periods, also bounded by the configured forced discharge deadline. Losing the actor, an urgent zone or recovery can require a release without a clear ray. The resulting tail shot stays latched off until an observed reset. A short disables charge and removes its speed penalty immediately. A missed decision with a nonzero charge is treated as a possible server-queued release.

Zone arrival retains S00's route and acceleration estimate. It reduces the walking speed only over the estimated remainder of the charge and a two-decision alignment allowance. A queued release instead reserves time to minimum charge plus a decision for server confirmation. This approximates the speed cap without predicting a full alternate movement trajectory. Normal travel and recovery priorities stay with S00. Official checker timings and synthetic action traces verify the mechanism. Placement and damage require independent game evaluation.
