# S01: threat-aware engagement

This is an independent implementation on S00 commit 19126d6516826c1de4e0e54c107c7bc4d6a23d81. No prior derived bot is an implementation source or runtime fallback.

## Evidence and scope

In the approximate local trace 40101, attacker 13 was absent from all observed mechs during 19–27.8 seconds. It caused 1080 HP of the 1215 HP damage after 20 seconds, all rear hits with an observed bearing. S00 kept aiming at a visible opponent. This is a behavior witness, not an official death log. S00 also marks partially reduced exposure as covered, suppressing return fire without eliminating exposure.

The user requests urgent substantial improvement. Ruling: S01 expands the former fixed-target-only experiment to permit a bounded reaction to verified incoming damage; keeping the target absolutely fixed would hide the dominant recorded threat. The cost is that official S01 measures an integrated combat change rather than a single weight.

## Required behavior

- Keep short-lived memory of observed player damage directions and attacker IDs. Do not assume hidden coordinates, ammo, cooldowns or intent. Distinguish current visible positions from bearing-only evidence.
- Repeated or meaningful rear attacks must affect facing even when a visible decoy is shootable. Turn toward the strongest recent threat long enough to acquire it, with bounded expiry and deterministic arbitration. Once the attacker is seen use its real position. Avoid perpetual turning to old events.
- Evaluate a bounded set of short-horizon ground movement candidates using actual velocity and acceleration, geometry and zone feasibility. Include the original S00 action and a stop candidate. Use visible projectile paths and known threats plus explicitly approximate bearing-only pressure. Candidate count at most 12, horizon initially 0.5 s. Do not invent exact hidden origin positions to claim cover.
- Preserve valid fire opportunities; target/facing, movement and fire must describe the same selected action. A facing override cannot silently retain a fire command for a discarded target.
- Full cover can suppress attacks for regeneration. Partial cover must allow legal return fire and must not pretend the remaining threat is gone. Zone urgency retains priority. No imported retreat/weapon logic.
- Leave resource policy, upgrades, and supported weapon kinds unchanged for S01. Laser support is a separate later candidate, if justified and within remaining budget.
- Preserve bounded runtime; fallback uses this own S00 logic only.

## Implementation record

S01 starts from the independent S00 controller specified above. The new decision code uses that controller, the official protocol and map API, the recorded hit directions, and elementary acceleration and relative-motion geometry. No other bot source, strategy package, binary, or external module is used.

- `threat.go` decays actual player damage and rear damage over 1.1 seconds. Rear damage totaling 3.5% of max HP opens a 1.35-second acquisition window. Candidate slots are ranked by decayed total plus rear damage, with lower ID winning exact ties. Observed actors supply their current position. Hidden attackers supply only a yaw.
- `controller.go` computes the existing movement proposal first, gives urgent zone movement precedence, resolves the selected engagement or crate, then performs the physical fire checks for that selection once. The original resource and upgrade implementations and supported weapons are unchanged.
- `evaluate.go` checks the original movement, stop and eight headings for ten 50 ms steps. Each step uses observed velocity, configured acceleration, the swept cylinder and the future circle. The eight nearest threatening projectile paths are evaluated in relative motion against the moving body, with gravity for mortar shells and static-impact clipping. Endpoint exposure uses up to three nearest reported actors. Bearing-only pressure rewards lateral displacement and supplies no cover test.
- `navigation.go` labels an exposed route to better cover as an escape. A stationary recovery hold requires zero current observed exposure and no priority bearing-only attacker.
- All numerical weights are hypotheses. The ground rollout rejects obstructed trajectories rather than simulating wall slides. It falls back to the original move on elevated supports, in the air, or if every candidate is infeasible. The model does not predict future target intent or hidden cooldowns. Mortar splash near an estimated impact uses an approximate radius without a fresh obstruction test.
